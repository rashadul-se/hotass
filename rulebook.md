# Compliance Rulebook: International Students in Montréal, Québec, Canada

*Prepared 25 November 2026. General information, not legal advice. Immigration and Québec rules change often. For anything that affects your status, confirm with your schools International Student Services (ISS) office or a licensed advisor (lawyer, Québec notary or RCIC) before acting.*

## How to use this rulebook

Each rule has an ID, a risk level and a one-line action. Rules are grouped into eight categories.

- 🔴 **Status-critical**: breaking it can cost you your study permit or your right to remain in Canada.
- 🟠 **Legal or financial**: breaking it can bring fines, debt, legal liability or lost opportunities.
- 🟡 **Institutional or good practice**: breaking it can bring discipline, lost privileges or reputational harm.

Categories: FED (federal), QC (provincial), SCH (schools), ASC (associations), CORP (corporate and employers), ENG (engineering ethics), IP (painting and intellectual property), UNI (student unions).

**Order of authority when rules conflict:** federal immigration and criminal law > Québec law > your school's regulations > association, union and employer rules. Always follow the strictest applicable rule.

## 1. FEDERAL (FED): IRCC, CBSA, CRA and federal law

**FED-1 🔴 Keep valid status.** Hold a valid passport and study permit (plus a valid CAQ in Québec). Apply to extend your study permit *before* it expires, and leave a generous margin (start 90 days ahead). Never stay or work on an expired document.

**FED-2 🔴 Study actively.** Stay enrolled at a designated learning institution (DLI), full-time unless an exception is approved, and make real academic progress. Your school reports enrolment to the government.

**FED-3 🔴 Changing school or program.** Tell IRCC when you change DLI, and check with ISS first whether you also need a new CAQ (Québec) or a new attestation letter. Do not switch first and sort the paperwork out later.

**FED-4 🔴 Work only as your permit allows.** Your permit must state a work condition, and you need a SIN. Off campus: up to 24 hours per week while classes are in session (counted across *all* jobs), unlimited hours during scheduled breaks. Do not start working before your program starts. Exceeding the cap is a breach of your conditions and can cost you your status and future permits.

**FED-5 🔴 Track your hours.** Keep a weekly log (employer, dates, hours). Self-employment counts toward the cap and you must be able to prove compliance.

**FED-6 🔴 No work while not studying.** If you take a leave, are suspended, or are expelled, you lose the basis for working. Stop work and call ISS immediately.

**FED-7 🟠 Mandatory co-op or internship.** Since 1 April 2026, post-secondary students no longer need a separate co-op work permit for a placement that is a required part of their program, provided they meet the conditions (including a valid study permit with an on-campus work condition). Get a written letter from your school confirming the placement is required. Secondary-level students still need a co-op work permit.

**FED-8 🟠 Remote work for a foreign employer.** Work done for an employer outside Canada does not count toward the 24-hour cap, but your study permit conditions still apply. Do not serve Canadian clients or Canadian-based entities under this rule.

**FED-9 🟠 Money.** Keep proof of financial capacity (tuition plus living costs). The financial requirement was raised in September 2025 and is updated periodically, so check the current figure on canada.ca.

**FED-10 🟠 Criminal law and admissibility.** Convictions (impaired driving, fraud, theft, assault and others) can make you inadmissible and end your stay. Peaceful assembly and expression are protected, but unlawful acts are not.

**FED-11 🟠 Taxes.** File a federal income tax return every year (even with no income, to access credits). Protect your SIN and never share it except with a legitimate employer or government body.

**FED-12 🟠 Political contributions.** Foreign nationals cannot contribute to federal parties, candidates or election campaigns.

**FED-13 🟠 Immigration advice.** Only lawyers, Québec notaries and licensed immigration consultants (RCIC) may charge for immigration advice. Never pay anyone who promises a job, a permit or a guaranteed result.

**FED-14 🟡 Plan for the post-graduation work permit (PGWP).** Language proof (English or French) is required at a level set by your program type. Bachelor's, master's and doctoral graduates are exempt from the field-of-study requirement; graduates of college or other non-degree programs must be in an eligible field. Confirm your program's eligibility *before you enrol*, apply within the deadline after you receive your completion letter, and keep valid language-test results.

**FED-15 🟡 Travel and re-entry.** Carry your passport, permit, CAQ, school enrolment letter and proof of funds. Declare goods (including artworks and equipment) honestly at the border.

## 2. PROVINCIAL (QC): Québec and Montréal law

**QC-1 🔴 CAQ.** Hold a valid Québec Acceptance Certificate (CAQ) for studies unless you are exempt (for example some short exchange stays). Your permit duration is aligned with your CAQ. Renew the CAQ before it expires, and expect to need a new one if you change level, program or institution. Québec's 2024 law on international students (Bill 74) lets the province tie some students to the institution it approved, so do not assume you can transfer freely.

**QC-2 🔴 Continuous health insurance.** You must be insured for your *entire* stay. If your country has a social-security agreement with Québec, you may qualify for RAMQ after a waiting period (usually three months), so you need interim cover. Otherwise use your school's group plan or private insurance. RAMQ generally does not cover dental, optometry or prescription drugs, so consider supplemental cover.

**QC-3 🟠 Labour rights apply to you.** Québec labour standards (minimum wage, pay, overtime, vacation, harassment protection, health and safety) apply regardless of immigration status. Report abuses to the CNESST. Your employer may not keep your passport, charge you a recruitment fee or make unlawful pay deductions.

**QC-4 🟠 Québec tax.** File a Québec return with Revenu Québec *in addition to* your federal return.

**QC-5 🟠 French Language Charter.** Workers have the right to work in French and clients have the right to be served in French. If you sell goods or services as a business (including selling art), public commercial signage, invoices and contracts must meet the Charter, with French present and predominant. Check with the OQLF.

**QC-6 🟠 Privacy (Law 25).** If a job, research project or club handles other people's personal information, follow Québec's privacy law: collect only what is needed, secure it, and report incidents.

**QC-7 🟠 Housing.** Read your lease before signing. Know your rights at the Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL) and Montréal's moving-day and noise rules. Do not pay a deposit by wire transfer to someone you have not met; Québec generally prohibits landlord deposits beyond the rent rules.

**QC-8 🟡 Local conduct.** Cannabis is legal from age 21 in Québec, alcohol from age 18. Follow Montréal bylaws on noise, waste sorting, public drinking and winter parking.

**QC-9 🟡 Staying after studies.** Québec's immigration pathways and French-language requirements change frequently. Check quebec.ca for the *current* programs and begin French study early.

## 3. SCHOOLS (SCH): universities, CEGEPs and colleges

**SCH-1 🔴 Academic integrity.** No plagiarism, contract cheating, fabricated data, or unauthorized AI use. Sanctions can reach expulsion, which ends your eligibility to study and work in Canada. If unsure whether a tool or collaboration is allowed, ask the instructor *in writing*.

**SCH-2 🔴 Full-time load and good standing.** Never drop below full-time, withdraw or take a leave without written clearance from ISS. Limited exceptions exist (for example a final term), but they must be confirmed.

**SCH-3 🟠 Give your school your documents.** Submit your new permit, CAQ and insurance proof promptly after each renewal. Keep your address and contact details current.

**SCH-4 🟠 Fees.** Pay tuition by the deadline. Understand refund schedules before withdrawing from a course.

**SCH-5 🟠 Conduct, harassment and sexual violence.** Québec law requires institutions to have a policy against sexual violence. Learn your school's code of conduct and reporting channels, and use them in good faith.

**SCH-6 🟠 Research ethics.** Any study involving human participants needs research-ethics board approval *before* you collect data. Handle data per your school's policy.

**SCH-7 🟠 Lab, studio and workshop safety.** Complete required safety training (including WHMIS) before using equipment or chemicals. Never work alone in hazardous settings.

**SCH-8 🟡 Use the right advisor.** ISS advisors are your first stop for status questions. They cannot give formal legal advice, but they know your school's reporting obligations.

**SCH-9 🟡 Keep your records.** Save transcripts, enrolment letters, fee receipts and every email with ISS and your department.

## 4. ASSOCIATIONS (ASC): professional bodies, clubs and community groups

**ASC-1 🟠 Professional titles.** Do not use "engineer" or "ing." (or any other regulated title) unless you are a member of the relevant order. Describe yourself as an "engineering student" or, where the OIQ allows it, "candidate to the engineering profession (CEP)".

**ASC-2 🟠 Join recognized groups.** Confirm a club is accredited by your school, has insurance, and has written bylaws before you hold an executive role. Officers can be personally liable for debts, safety failures and misused funds.

**ASC-3 🟠 Events.** Obtain venue approval and any required alcohol permits, follow capacity and safety rules, and never haze or coerce participation.

**ASC-4 🟠 Volunteering vs. work.** Genuine volunteering for non-profits is generally fine, but unpaid activity that replaces paid labour can be treated as work. Check with ISS before taking an unpaid role with a business.

**ASC-5 🟡 Codes of conduct.** Student chapters of professional societies (for example IEEE or engineering societies) have codes of ethics. Treat them as binding on you, in addition to law.

**ASC-6 🟡 Money and transparency.** Keep receipts, minutes and a written budget. Two people should approve any large expense.

**ASC-7 🟠 Political money.** Do not accept or send donations to political parties or campaigns on behalf of a group if foreign funding rules could apply. When in doubt, don't.

## 5. CORPORATE (CORP): employers, internships and freelancing

**CORP-1 🔴 Pre-hire check.** Before accepting work, confirm: (a) your permit allows it, (b) the hours fit under the 24-hour cap (or the break/placement exception applies), (c) you have a SIN, (d) you have a written offer or contract.

**CORP-2 🟠 Unpaid internships.** In Québec, unpaid internships are generally lawful only when they are a supervised part of an educational program. Otherwise they may be unlawful and can also breach your work conditions. Get a school letter.

**CORP-3 🟠 Confidentiality and trade secrets.** Read and keep copies of every NDA. Do not take client files, source code or designs to school projects or personal portfolios without written permission.

**CORP-4 🟠 Who owns what you create.** Under Canadian copyright law, works made in the course of employment normally belong to the employer unless a contract says otherwise. Negotiate written carve-outs for pre-existing work and personal projects (including art).

**CORP-5 🟠 Controlled goods and export controls.** Work touching defence, aerospace or certain technologies may require registration or a security assessment before a foreign national can access it. Ask the employer's designated official before you see any such material.

**CORP-6 🟠 Anti-corruption and sanctions.** Never offer or accept bribes or improper gifts, and follow conflict-of-interest rules. Respect Canadian sanctions and export rules when handling technology or clients abroad.

**CORP-7 🟠 Data protection.** Follow the employer's security rules. Do not store client or personal data on personal devices or personal cloud accounts.

**CORP-8 🟠 Workplace safety.** You may refuse work that you reasonably believe is dangerous, and you must report incidents. Insist on training and protective equipment.

**CORP-9 🔴 Self-employment and freelancing.** Freelancing for Canadian clients counts as work and counts toward your hours. Incorporating a company does *not* create work authorization. If your revenue exceeds the federal and Québec small-supplier threshold (currently $30,000 over four consecutive quarters), you must register for GST and QST. Confirm current thresholds with the CRA and Revenu Québec.

**CORP-10 🟡 Scams.** Reject any employer who asks you to pay to be hired, to cash cheques for them, or to move money. Verify employers independently.

## 6. ENGINEERING ETHICS (ENG): Québec's regulated profession

In Québec, engineering is regulated by the *Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec* (OIQ) under the Engineers Act, the Professional Code and the Code of ethics of engineers. Students are not members, but the standards below shape every placement, capstone and career step.

**ENG-1 🔴 Do not practise unlicensed.** Only OIQ members may use the title and perform the reserved activities. As a student or intern you work *under the direct supervision* of a licensed engineer who takes professional responsibility.

**ENG-2 🔴 Never seal or sign.** Plans and documents may be sealed and signed only by authorized engineers. Never imitate a seal or sign for someone else.

**ENG-3 🔴 Public safety comes first.** If you believe work endangers public safety, tell the responsible persons and, where required, the Order. Do not stay silent because of seniority, deadlines or fear for your status. Seek advice from ISS or an OIQ contact if you fear retaliation.

**ENG-4 🟠 Honesty and integrity.** No dishonest or doubtful practices: no fabricated test data, false credentials, misleading reports, or manipulated results in labs or capstone projects.

**ENG-5 🟠 Competence.** Accept only tasks you are competent to perform, and ask for supervision and training when you are not.

**ENG-6 🟠 Confidentiality.** Treat client and employer information as confidential, as an engineer would under professional-secrecy rules, except where law requires disclosure.

**ENG-7 🟠 Conflicts of interest.** Declare any financial or personal interest that could affect your judgment, and refuse gifts that could compromise independence.

**ENG-8 🟡 Standards and sustainability.** Apply relevant standards and codes (CSA, National Building Code, Québec construction codes), consider environmental and social impact, and document design decisions.

**ENG-9 🟡 French and licensing path.** OIQ members must comply with the Charter of the French Language, and the admission process assesses language. Graduates of foreign programs must obtain degree equivalency (including a credential report) and complete the OIQ process. Students in an accredited Québec program can apply to the OIQ after completing 60 credits and then use the protected title "CEP" with supervision limits. Licensed status in another country or province does *not* authorize practice in Québec.

## 7. PAINTING AND INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY (IP)

**IP-1 🟠 Copyright is automatic.** Copyright in an original painting exists from creation, without registration, and generally lasts the author's life plus 70 years. You own it unless you are an employee making it in the course of employment, or you assign it in writing. Selling the physical painting does *not* sell the copyright.

**IP-2 🟠 Moral rights.** You have rights of attribution, integrity and association with your work. They cannot be assigned but can be waived in writing, so read every contract for waivers. Owners of your work should not distort or mutilate it in ways that harm your honour or reputation.

**IP-3 🟠 Respect others' works.** Do not copy, trace or closely imitate protected paintings, photographs or characters for sale or public display. Canada uses "fair dealing", *not* US "fair use": it covers only specific purposes (research, private study, education, parody, satire, criticism, review, news reporting) and must be fair. Use public-domain, licensed or original reference images.

**IP-4 🟠 AI tools.** Follow your school's rules on AI use and disclose it when required. Do not prompt tools to reproduce a living artist's style or specific work. Copyright protection for output with little human authorship is uncertain in Canada, so keep records of your own creative contribution.

**IP-5 🟠 Commissions and sales.** Use a written agreement: scope, price, deadlines, licence to reproduce, who owns copyright, moral-rights terms, and cancellation. Issue invoices and record income. **Selling art in Canada is business activity and can count as work or self-employment for immigration purposes.** Confirm with ISS before selling regularly, and count the hours.

**IP-6 🟠 Taxes on art income.** Declare art income on your federal and Québec returns. Register for GST/QST once you pass the small-supplier threshold (see CORP-9).

**IP-7 🟠 Murals and public art.** You need the property owner's written consent and any required municipal or borough authorization before painting a mural or installing public art in Montréal. Unauthorized painting can be vandalism.

**IP-8 🟠 Trademarks, logos and characters.** Do not include brands, logos or licensed characters in artwork you sell without permission.

**IP-9 🟡 Cultural respect.** Seek permission and guidance before using Indigenous or other communities' sacred, ceremonial or culturally specific imagery, and credit sources.

**IP-10 🟡 Prove authorship.** Keep dated process photos, sketches, source files and sales records. A copyright notice (© name, year) is helpful. Registration with the Canadian Intellectual Property Office is optional but useful in disputes.

**IP-11 🟠 School and research IP.** Read your school's IP policy. Students usually own their coursework, but sponsored research, lab work and co-op placements may assign IP to the school or sponsor. Theses usually involve a licence to the library or repository.

**IP-12 🟠 Inventions.** Disclose a patentable invention to your school's tech-transfer office or an agent *before* publishing it. Canada allows a limited grace period for your own disclosure, but other countries may not.

**IP-13 🟡 Gallery and consignment contracts.** Record commission percentage, duration, insurance, who pays shipping, pricing control and return terms in writing.

**IP-14 🟡 Studio safety and shipping.** Use ventilation, label solvents, dispose of paints and solvents as hazardous waste (not down the drain), and follow WHMIS for pigments such as cadmium or lead. When bringing art across the border, declare it and keep invoices.

## 8. STUDENT UNIONS (UNI)

**UNI-1 🟠 Accredited associations.** Québec's Act respecting the accreditation and financing of students' associations lets an accredited association be the recognized representative of the students it covers and collect fees from members. Know which association represents you and read its bylaws.

**UNI-2 🟠 Fees and opt-outs.** Compulsory fees are approved through the association's referendum rules, but some fees are optional with a short opt-out window (at some universities, around the start of the fall and winter terms). Check your invoice and your school's deadline, and opt out in writing *before* the deadline if you want to.

**UNI-3 🔴 Never opt out of essential health insurance.** If your association or school runs the mandatory health plan, do not opt out until your replacement coverage is confirmed in writing (see QC-2). A gap in coverage can breach a condition of your stay.

**UNI-4 🟠 Your participation rights.** As a member you can attend meetings, vote and run for office under the bylaws. If you accept a paid union job, confirm that it counts as authorized on-campus work and that your permit conditions allow it.

**UNI-5 🟠 Officer duties.** Elected officers owe duties of loyalty and care, must follow the budget and bylaws, and should record decisions in minutes. Misusing funds can lead to civil or criminal liability.

**UNI-6 🔴 Strikes, protests and boycotts.** You have a right to peaceful expression and assembly. A strike vote does not by itself remove your right to attend classes, and courts have sometimes ordered classes to remain accessible. Do not take part in blockades, intimidation, property damage or harassment, because criminal charges or convictions can affect your status. If classes are disrupted, keep your enrolment active and ask ISS how it affects you.

**UNI-7 🟠 Political donations.** Do not donate to political parties or campaigns and do not channel union money to them if you are a foreign national (see FED-12).

**UNI-8 🟡 Elections and campaigning.** Campaign honestly, follow the elections code, and never trade votes for benefits.

**UNI-9 🟡 Complaints.** Use the association's grievance process, the school's ombudsperson and, for discrimination or harassment, the Commission des droits de la personne et des droits de la jeunesse.

## 9. CROSS-CUTTING TOOLS

### 9.1 Your compliance evidence file (keep for at least 7 years)

- Passport, study permit, CAQ and every previous version
- School enrolment letters, transcripts and fee receipts
- Proof of health insurance for every period of your stay
- Weekly work-hours log, pay stubs, contracts and SIN confirmation
- Letters confirming required placements or internships
- Tax returns (federal and Québec) and notices of assessment
- Art sales invoices, contracts and copyright records
- Union and club fee receipts and opt-out confirmations
- Written advice from ISS or advisors

### 9.2 Calendar of recurring deadlines

- **90 days before permit expiry:** start the study permit extension.
- **Before CAQ expiry:** renew the CAQ (check your school's timeline).
- **Start of each term:** confirm full-time enrolment and insurance.
- **Early September and January:** check student-fee opt-out windows.
- **By the filing deadline each spring:** file federal and Québec tax returns.
- **Final year:** confirm PGWP eligibility, book a language test and apply within the deadline after your completion letter.

### 9.3 Escalation guide

- Status or permit question: school ISS first, then a lawyer, Québec notary or RCIC.
- Employment abuse: CNESST.
- Tax: CRA and Revenu Québec.
- Engineering practice or title: OIQ.
- Copyright or trademark: a lawyer or the Canadian Intellectual Property Office.
- Housing: Tribunal administratif du logement.
- Discrimination or harassment: school ombudsperson and the Québec human rights commission.
- Emergency: 911.

### 9.4 Rules most likely to have changed: verify before relying

- Study permit caps and attestation requirements (2026 target is about 155,000 new study permits nationally).
- Financial-capacity amount and PGWP language levels and eligible-field lists.
- Québec programs for staying after study and any new Bill 74 regulations.
- Small-supplier GST/QST thresholds and alcohol/cannabis rules.
